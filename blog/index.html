2/7/26

I am getting sick and tired of the amount of times that I see another youtube video upload about how some company is suddenly moving to a subscription for everything. Everybody has a right to be mad about subscriptions, but there's something very specific that I want to complain about here.

This idea that we as a society think that it's socially acceptable for corporations to use a subscription for some of the most basic things, like owning a laptop, is absolutely ridiculous to me. I think that if it received more coverage from the media, there would be a metric ton of outcry about it, but the media simply doesn't cover it. They don't want to cover it because of how slow-rolling it is. It's boring. Nobody wants to hear about how Hewlett-Packard is now messing around with the idea of a subscription TO USE A LAPTOP rather than buying one because it doesn't affect them. Nobody wants to hear about how horrible the financies of such a subscription are, because of how quickly the amount you pay outpaces the cost of the actual machine itself.

I realize that not everybody wants to use some laptop with a Coffee-Lake CPU from 2018 or older because of how easy it is to fix. I understand how alot of people just want to use a computer, and that's it. As much as I try to encourage people buying used, it keeps the prices relatively low for me when I want to do things like upgrade a T480, so I'm really not trying to sound like I'm some person that's not taking advantage of how little the used market is actually explored. But even if we look at the used vs. new argument, the end result is still the same; you now own a computer that you can use for something. Hewlett-Packard is stripping that right to own the laptop you pay for by allowing people to use a subscription for a laptop that has bad hinges, a terrible keyboard, and a fragile frame that would desintegrate if dropped. To be clear, Hewlett-Packard is not the only one that is doing this, but they're the ones that incited this rant.

Certainly more people think that this is bad, right? Surely, because there has been outcry about subscriptions before. But nobody is saying anything about laptops, because they're getting too expensive these days, which actually brings me to another tangent about how horrible laptops are these days. I cannot for the life of me justify spending so much money on any new laptop, let alone the ones that are trying to be both ultra thin and powerful. As much as I may not like the software, Macintosh does it perfectly. Their silicon is still unmatched for laptops to this day, but for people to chase the same thing with an x86 processor has to be the easiest philosophy for me to reject. Not every laptop has to be a huge, thick laptop, and I can easily get behind that, but the problem arises when you try to look at the market, you have to choose either gaming or ultra thin portability because your local Best Buy doesn't sell anything else.

I've had my experience with huge laptops. I still to this day have an Acer Predator Helios 300 from 2018 that comparitively weighs a ton, but it's to the point where ports are just being removed willy nilly because it doesn't fit the philosophy of having an ultra portable and powerful machine. Why is there no middleground these days? Why is it that if I want a decently built laptop from Best Buy I have to get one with a dGPU? I firmly understand that there are many laptops online that do not fit this stereotype, but I am not the typical consumer when it comes to computers. I understand that used ThinkPads and Framework Laptops are the magnum opus of laptops, but others do not. Why do they have to be sold junk? You could argue that it's the responsibility of the consumer to do research, and while I would agree, it's also the responsibility of the storefront to have alot of variance for competition. If somebody can't find the laptop they researched in a local store, why would they order one online? It doesn't really make much sense for them, especially because it's far easier to trust a store because you walk in with money and you walk out with a computer.

I don't think that there is any single noble company that could outdo the tragedy of the Macbook. It was too appealing and thin, and now the consumer is paying the price of mimicry. I'm sick of it. I'm sick and tired of seeing all the advertisements about how thin and light a computer is, because it truly doesn't matter. Unless there's some disability in play here, I truly cannot understand why something like a T480 wouldn't be seen as a good thin laptop. This race to make computers paper thin will only make portable computing worse, and I will not stand for it. If that means that I'll have to keep using old computers or pay the premium price for niche brands who endorse repairability, then so be it. I will pay the price.
